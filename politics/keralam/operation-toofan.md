# Operation Toofan — Anti-Narcotics Drive (Keralam)
{: .no_toc }

## Summary

Operation Toofan is the UDF government's statewide anti-narcotics drive,
launched June 2, 2026 and now in its second phase ("Toofan 2.0," from
September 28). The launch and headline results are tracked as
[entry #3](achievements/2026.html#entry-3) and
[entry #6](achievements/2026.html#entry-6) in UDF Government
Achievements; one arrest arising from it is tracked as a controversy in
[entry #16](controversies/2026.html#entry-16) of UDF
Government Controversies. This page is a separate, cross-checked look at
recurring criticism of the
operation's design and execution — privacy, treatment capacity, targeting,
and how enforcement and rehabilitation are actually being weighted against
each other — rather than a chronological log of events.

## Notes

### Privacy and public exposure


<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 1. Media visibility and identity protection for people who use drugs

*Ongoing since June 2026*

The operation is deliberately social-media-driven — Kerala Police posts
raid, seizure, and awareness videos, and runs public tip-off lines — which
has amplified some episodes far beyond police control. In one case, a
local Instagram account's video of alleged public drug use drew over 2
million views overnight after being shared and tagged by popular
accounts, including Kerala Police's own page. Separately, a July 2026
investigative report on Perumbavoor found that raids and searches in
migrant-worker settlements and labour camps were being filmed and
circulated widely, with the filming itself sometimes framed as "civic
responsibility" — raising concerns about the dignity and identity exposure
of a group that is economically essential but socially peripheral,
linguistically isolated, and poorly placed to contest how it's portrayed.
I did not find reporting of a specific policy proposal to keep referred
users' identities confidential (e.g. anonymized rehab referral) — that
appears to be a real gap rather than a live policy debate.

- [The Most Visible Suspects: Operation Toofan, Perumbavoor and Migrant Workers (Sabrang India)](https://sabrangindia.in/the-most-visible-suspects-operation-toofan-perumbavoor-and-migrant-workers/)
- [Operation Toofan: How Kerala govt is taking anti-drug fight from police stations to social media (ThePrint)](https://theprint.in/india/operation-toofan-how-kerala-govt-is-taking-anti-drug-fight-from-police-stations-to-social-media/2970477/)

### Awareness and rehabilitation capacity


<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 2. Awareness programme: activity claims vs. measured effectiveness

*Ongoing since June 2026*

The government's own figures for the "Toofan Warriors" awareness wing are
large — 10,295 awareness classes claimed in phase 1 alone, alongside a
claimed 30% drop in drug abuse and peddling statewide. But independent
reporting frames the operation overall as one that "is yielding results
from an enforcement perspective but fails to address drug abuse as a
pressing public health concern" — i.e. the criticism is less that no
awareness activity happened, and more that high activity counts aren't
shown to translate into treatment uptake or behaviour change. For
context only — this is a **different program under the previous LDF
government**, not Operation Toofan — a 2022 anti-drug campaign drew a
structurally similar critique from a Kerala State Mental Health Authority
psychiatrist, who said no mental health professionals, social scientists,
or sociologists were consulted in designing it. Flagging it here only
because the same design gap (awareness campaigns built without mental
health expertise) recurs across governments, not because it's evidence
about Toofan itself.

**Sources:**
- [Operation Toofan 2.0 launched, drug abuse and peddling down 30 per cent in Keralam, says Chennithala (ThePrint)](https://theprint.in/india/operation-toofan-2-0-launched-drug-abuse-and-peddling-down-30-per-cent-in-keralam-chennithala/3055373/)
- [Kerala's Operation Toofan: arrests rise, but addiction care struggles to keep up (The News Minute)](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/operation-toofan-kerala-police-struggle-to-deal-with-addicts)

<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 3. Toofan Care: treatment capacity vs. arrest volume

*Ongoing since June 2026*

Toofan Care, the rehabilitation arm, has partnership agreements with
roughly 35 hospitals. But as of the most recent reporting, only about 100
people were actually undergoing hospital-based treatment through it —
against 11,776+ arrests in cases registered under the same operation over
the same period. Addiction psychiatrist Dr. Mohan Roy is quoted noting
that arrested users usually aren't even screened for which substance they
consumed, which blocks proper treatment planning: "It is not always clear
what substance the particular arrested user has consumed." The
100-hospital-vs-11,776-arrests gap is the clearest, most concretely
sourced version of the "Toofan Care isn't keeping pace" criticism.

**Source:** [Kerala's Operation Toofan: arrests rise, but addiction care struggles to keep up (The News Minute)](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/operation-toofan-kerala-police-struggle-to-deal-with-addicts)


</div>

<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 4. Custody and hospital access for arrested users

*Ongoing since June 2026*

The 35-hospital tie-up under Toofan Care does not appear to extend, in
practice, to people arrested for possessing small quantities for personal
use — they're mostly processed through ordinary custody rather than
routed into treatment. Police and prison officials describe managing
withdrawal symptoms and violent episodes in lockups without clinical
support: a jail superintendent described inmates who "would turn violent
due to withdrawal symptoms" and need isolation and sedation for days, and
officers at Maradu police station reported a specific incident of an
MDMA-possession detainee hitting his head against walls and attacking
officers. Dr. Mohan Roy's recommendation is to treat such cases inside
jail premises with proper facilities, rather than relying on sedation, and
to train police, excise, and prison staff to recognize and handle
withdrawal.

**Source:** [Kerala's Operation Toofan: arrests rise, but addiction care struggles to keep up (The News Minute)](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/operation-toofan-kerala-police-struggle-to-deal-with-addicts)


</div>

### Enforcement targeting


<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 5. Peddlers vs. users: who is actually being arrested

*Ongoing since June 2026*

Through September 1, 2026, cases involving small (personal-use) quantities
outnumbered cases involving commercial (trafficking) quantities by roughly
27 to 1 — 3,127 small-quantity cases against 116 commercial-quantity
cases. That ratio is the concrete, sourced version of the "target
peddlers, not users" concern: enforcement volume is dominated by
possession-level arrests, not network-level trafficking cases, even
though the operation's own stated goal (per its June 2026 launch
framing) was to "overhaul the drug mafia," not simply arrest users or
low-level dealers.

**Source:** [Kerala's Operation Toofan: arrests rise, but addiction care struggles to keep up (The News Minute)](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/operation-toofan-kerala-police-struggle-to-deal-with-addicts)


</div>

<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 6. Official rhetoric on reform vs. the execution gap

*September 2026*

The government's own framing already lines up with "reform, not shame."
Announcing Toofan 2.0, Home Minister Ramesh Chennithala described a
three-pronged strategy run in parallel: strict enforcement and
imprisonment for traffickers, identifying people affected by addiction,
and rehabilitating them — "We want to rehabilitate the people who were
actually using drugs." The gap isn't in the stated policy; it's that this
distinction doesn't yet show up cleanly in outcomes (see entries #3–#5
above) — a say–do gap rather than the government rejecting a
reform-over-punishment framing outright.

**Sources:**
- [Chennithala: Operation Toofan will be free of politics (Deccan Chronicle)](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/kerala/chennithala-operation-toofan-will-be-free-of-politics-1980699)

### Underlying mental health context


<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 7. Mental health access as a possible driver — plausible, not yet documented for Toofan specifically

*Background context*

{: .note }
Unlike the entries above, no report I found draws a direct, explicit line
from Kerala's mental health care gap to Operation Toofan's arrest
population, or names specific professions like engineers or doctors as an
at-risk group in that context. This entry lays out the underlying data
that makes the theory plausible, not a confirmed causal claim.

Kerala has only 5.53 mental health beds per 10,000 people (2018 data),
against a rising burden: mental illness prevalence rose from 194 to 300
per 100,000 people between 2002 and 2018, and the National Mental Health
Survey (2015–16) recorded an 11.36% overall prevalence of mental
disorders in the state, with substance use disorders at 4.82%. A
Kerala-specific study of 400 IT professionals working from home (surveyed
Dec 2022–Mar 2023) found 24% anxiety and 11% stress prevalence, with
substance abuse — alongside night shifts and inadequate rest —
significantly linked to poor mental health outcomes in that group. More
broadly (and predating Toofan — this data is from a 2025 report, under
the previous government), the beneficiary count for de-addiction services
in Kerala rose from 4,239 in 2020–21 to 10,385 in 2022–23, with one
Kochi-based source describing drug use as having "spread across all
sectors, from IT professionals to school kids." None of this is
Toofan-specific evidence; it's the background rate at which Kerala's
mental health and addiction-services gap appears to be biting across
professional classes, which is the closest verifiable support for the
"people turn to drugs for relief where mental health care is
inaccessible" hypothesis.

**Sources:**
- [Is Kerala's mental health burden outpacing its care capacity? (The South First)](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/is-keralas-mental-health-burden-outpacing-its-care-capacity/)
- [Prevalence and determinants of depression, anxiety, and stress among IT professionals working from home (International Journal of Occupational Safety and Health)](https://nepjol.info/index.php/IJOSH/article/view/72275)
- *(2025 report, predates Toofan/UDF government)* [Kerala on drugs: school, college students are using MDMA, going to rehabs (ThePrint, April 2025)](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/gods-own-country-is-high-on-mdma-kerala-school-college-students-flood-rehabs/2574881/)


</div>

### The big picture


<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 8. Sequencing: parallel by design, enforcement-weighted in practice

*Ongoing since June 2026*

The government's stated position is explicitly parallel, not sequential —
Chennithala's three-pronged strategy (entry #6) runs enforcement,
identification, and rehabilitation together, and Toofan 2.0 added its
grama-sabha/Kudumbashree community layer and expanded hospital
partnerships alongside continued raids, not after them. In practice,
though, independent reporting shows the actual weighting skews heavily
toward enforcement: arrest and seizure numbers are large, tracked in real
time, and heavily publicized, while the care and rehabilitation side lags
far behind in measured throughput (entry #3) and isn't reported with
anything like the same rigor. So the honest answer to "which should come
first, or how should they be weighted" is: officially parallel, actually
enforcement-weighted — and that one gap in measurement and
infrastructure build-out is the common root of the targeting (#5),
capacity (#3–#4), and visibility (#1) criticisms above, more than any of
them being a separate, unrelated failure.

- [Kerala's Operation Toofan: arrests rise, but addiction care struggles to keep up (The News Minute)](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/operation-toofan-kerala-police-struggle-to-deal-with-addicts)
