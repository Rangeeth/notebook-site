# UDF Government Controversies — Keralam (2026)
{: .no_toc }

{% include last_updated.html %}

## Summary

A running, chronological log of controversies and criticism directed at the
UDF government (CM V.D. Satheesan) since it took office after the 2026
Kerala assembly election. Most entries describe allegations made by the
opposition, critics, or press reporting — not established fact or court
findings — so read descriptions as "X alleged Y", not "Y happened".

#### 1. Pre-result Mangalore chartered flight row

*Early May 2026*

Before the assembly election results were declared, opposition parties
alleged that V.D. Satheesan took a private chartered flight to Mangalore
for undisclosed meetings with senior NDA and Adani Group figures, prompting
collusion accusations. Reported specifics: a passenger manifest for a
May 1, 2026 charter from Kochi to Mangaluru listed just two passengers —
Satheesan and K. Rejikumar. In Mangaluru, Satheesan reportedly met JD(S)
leaders (the BJP's Karnataka ally) at a reception, allegedly without
informing the Karnataka Congress leadership beforehand, as is customary
for an out-of-state leader's visit; photos of the meeting later
circulated on social media. One outlet additionally reported that
Rejikumar, his co-passenger, had separately been questioned by the SIT
investigating the Sabarimala temple gold theft case (the same case behind
[entry #4](#pradeep-pleader-resignation)) — a claim carried by a single source and not
independently corroborated here.

#### 2. Cabinet formation & regional imbalance row

*May 2026*

Soon after the UDF took office, friction broke out over portfolio
allocation: the IUML got five posts while smaller allies were anxious about
which departments they would receive. The Latin Catholic Church, whose
community has four UDF MLAs but no minister, objected to Fisheries and
Education going to the IUML and stayed away from the May 18 swearing-in.
The Hindu's report also describes allies objecting that some districts
(e.g. Kollam) got several ministers while others got none; that detail couldn't be corroborated elsewhere, so treat it as single-sourced.

#### 3. Minister Sunny Joseph's brother-in-law named to his personal staff

*June 2026*

Electricity Minister and KPCC president Sunny Joseph faced nepotism
allegations after his brother-in-law Benny Thomas — husband of Joseph's
sister, a Kannur DCC office-bearer and former Ulikkal panchayat president —
was appointed his Additional Private Secretary in early June. CPI(M)
supporters compared it to the resignations of E.P. Jayarajan (2016) and
K.T. Jaleel (2021) over relatives' appointments; the minister's office
cited Thomas's political record. Thomas resigned around June 7, and Joseph
defended the appointment, saying there was no legal issue. This was a
political appointment to a minister's personal staff; the sources reviewed
don't show any wider appointments of relatives or bypassing of PSC
recruitment.

#### 4. K.B. Pradeep special pleader resignation
{: #pradeep-pleader-resignation }

*June 2026 (resigned June 13)*

#### 5. PM-SHRI education scheme U-turn

*June 2026*

In June the CM announced the state would continue with the Centre's
PM-SHRI education scheme, which the UDF had opposed while in opposition,
but with conditions, and set up a Cabinet subcommittee to review it. The
Left criticized
the reversal, noting only about a year remained on the scheme's original
2027 timeline.

{: .note }
**Update (October 3–4, 2026):** the government has reportedly put the
scheme on hold. The pause, the suspension of the Cabinet subcommittee's
work and the Muslim League's strong opposition are reported by Reporter
Live and SouthLive (October 3) as well as Onmanorama (October 4).
Onmanorama alone adds that the state says it can't withdraw from the MoU
signed by the previous LDF government and would implement it
conditionally, that there is a front-level understanding of "no need to
rush" and that it will proceed only after political consensus, and that the
CM and Education Minister N. Shamsudeen denied having taken a final decision
to abandon the scheme — those details are single-sourced.

#### 6. "Land Reforms 2.0" & asset privatization

*June 2026*

The revised 2026–27 state budget, presented June 19, cut the plan outlay
by about ₹5,380 crore (from ₹35,750 crore to ₹30,370 crore) and announced
a restructuring of KIIFB through an expert committee. "Land Reforms 2.0"
("Second Land Reform") is the budget's own name for a review of land
laws, not an opposition label. Former revenue minister K. Rajan said the
land-law changes would pave the way for large-scale transfer of land to
corporates, and Opposition Leader Pinarayi Vijayan and the CPI(M)
separately alleged the budget pursued privatisation of state assets — these
are the opposition's characterizations, not established findings.

#### 8. Nipah response row & "Eranamkettavan" remark

*June 2026*

A single Nipah case was confirmed in Kozhikode on June 11 — a 43-year-old
man from Feroke. WHO's June 25 notice lists one confirmed case, no deaths,
no secondary cases and 104 contacts under monitoring; the patient recovered
and was discharged in early August. The opposition LDF nonetheless accused
the government of failing to control the spread of diseases (Nipah, West
Nile, amoebic meningoencephalitis and others) and walked out of the
Assembly on June 22 after the Speaker denied an adjournment motion on the
issue. Health Minister K. Muraleedharan, who said it was more practical to
coordinate from Thiruvananthapuram than Kozhikode, also drew criticism when
an earlier speech from his time in opposition resurfaced on social media —
in which he said, roughly, that the land would be ruined if an "unlucky"
man (*eranamkettavan*) ruled it, alongside a remark about
previously unheard-of diseases; he later clarified it. Separately, ThePrint
reported three Shigella deaths in Kozhikode (two children aged 3 and 4, and
a 59-year-old woman) among 135 confirmed cases since January — unrelated to
Nipah.

{: .note }
An earlier version of this entry said the outbreak had killed "close to a
hundred people including children." That was wrong: no Nipah deaths were
reported. The figure likely conflates the roughly 100 contacts traced with
fatalities.

#### 9. Local body fund cuts

*June – July 2026*

The revised budget cut local-body plan funds by about ₹1,533 crore. In the
Assembly on July 1, LDF MLAs (CPI's G.R. Anil moved the motion) accused the
CM of misleading the House about the cut; the CM blamed the previous
government, saying it had budgeted far more than was available. Opposition
Leader Pinarayi Vijayan later led a march to the Secretariat (July 30–31),
saying there was "absolutely no justification" for withholding the funds
and that — as he put it — no first instalment had been released even four
months into the government's term.

#### 10. Election Commissioner appointment controversy

*July 2026*

The Cabinet decided on June 24 to appoint N. Seshadrinathan — who retired as Principal District and Sessions Judge, Lakshadweep, and was serving at the Irinjalakuda Family Court — as State Election Commissioner (Kerala Kaumudi, Asianet, ThePrint). The Governor's approval was reported around July 1 (Onmanorama dates it July 2) and he took charge on July 7. KPCC general secretary P.M. Niyas objected, alleging links with Sangh Parivar organisations (including that he is an office-bearer of the lawyers' body Bharatheeya Abhibhashaka Parishad) and saying the party hadn't been consulted; Minister K.M. Shaji said it was a collective Cabinet decision. Only Onmanorama reports that CPI(M) state secretary M.V. Govindan raised similar concerns and that the IUML expressed reservations. These are the objectors' allegations, not established fact.

#### 13. Sabarimala judicial interference row

*July 2026*

Devaswom Minister K. Muraleedharan said in the Assembly (around July 2)
that neither the Devaswom Board nor the government has any real role in
Sabarimala's administration because the High Court decides even small

government is not here to clash with the court" and that nothing he said was
defiance or disrespect. No named critic of the original remark appears in the sources reviewed.

#### 16. Operation Toofan MDMA bust & CM's social media team row
{: #toofan-mdma-bust }

*August 2026*

An anti-narcotics operation ("Operation Toofan") led to the arrest of
Youth Congress activists — including K.H. Mohammed Hussain (also referred
to as Huzain Muzafir) and Abdul Salam — at Kalikkadavu, Kasaragod, over a
synthetic MDMA seizure. CPI(M) state secretary M.V. Govindan and Left
organizations alleged the accused were core members of V.D. Satheesan's
personal social media team, and accused the CMO of pressuring Meta to
suppress critical coverage. Satheesan acknowledged knowing the individuals
but denied they were part of any official social media cell.

slowed significantly under the UDF: he said 308 of the 410 houses had been
concreted before the change of government but only nine more in the UDF's
first 70 days, blamed in part on "unnecessary transfers" of officials with
critical responsibilities, and said 149 families were still waiting (Awaz
The Voice). No government reply and no independent count of houses concreted were found, so this is an allegation, not an established fact. The baseline
was already behind schedule: on March 28, under the LDF and during the
Model Code of Conduct, Onmanorama reported more than 128 of the 178 first-phase houses inaugurated on March 1 still incomplete, with an official hoping for completion by April 15. That exact count is single-sourced; The News Minute (April 3) and IANS (April 17) describe the same picture with different details — plumbing and infrastructure unfinished, only about 40 houses ready per IANS, and a missed pre-Vishu promise. On September
10, Agriculture Minister T. Siddique said 178 keys had been handed over
with 177 families living there, about 110 more houses were due by
end-September, all 410 houses by October 31 and amenities by December, with
roughly 810 workers on site and a prepared weekly work schedule; he blamed rain for slight delays in painting (Onmanorama and Deccan Chronicle). The end-September target for about 110 houses has not been independently confirmed as met.

**Congress's Wayanad house fund — a party matter, not a government one.**
This predates the UDF taking office. The Congress and Youth Congress had
promised 100 houses for landslide survivors and run a fundraising drive;
the Congress acquired about 3.25 acres at Kunnampatta and laid the foundation stone on February 26 (The News Minute, Deccan Chronicle); Onmanorama described the project as having "nothing materialised" as of February 9, and by April only preliminary work had been done (The News Minute). In
April, KPCC president Sunny Joseph disclosed ₹5.38 crore received in a
joint account held with Satheesan, plus ₹1.05 crore collected by the Youth
Congress (reports differ on whether the latter is included in the former),
spent on land in two phases — about 3 acres 24.5 cents for ₹3.68 crore and
2 acres 15 cents for a little over ₹2.5 crore — with ₹97 lakh added from KPCC funds (The South First instead describes phase 1 as covering over five acres, so acreage reports differ). CPI(M) questioned whether the figures matched the scale of the
campaign (The Federal, April 7). More specific allegations — for instance that the account is missing from candidates' election
affidavits and the AICC balance sheet — come from a Deshabhimani report that couldn't be opened, so they are unverified here. No finding of misappropriation by any authority was found. CPI(M) was still questioning the
Congress's housing delay in early June, calling the February foundation-stone
ceremony a face-saving exercise (Hans India, June 6).

#### 28. Protest clashes and "selective policing" allegations
{: #protest-clashes-policing }

*September 26 – October 5, 2026*

A run of confrontations over the Youth Congress's campaign against Chief
Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar has produced allegations that police
treat the Left and the BJP's youth wing differently. Timeline:

- **Sept 26:** Youth Congress workers showed black flags at Union Home
  Minister Amit Shah's convoy in Kollam, reportedly breaching the security
  cordon (the BJP alleged a security lapse). Reports differ on what
  happened to the five Youth Congress activists involved: The South First
  says detained, Reporter Live and ThePrint say arrested.
- **Sept 28:** Minister O.J. Janeesh, who is also Youth Congress state
  president, posted video of the protest and then removed it. Reporter Live
  said the removal followed the CM's displeasure, and Onmanorama (Oct 1)
  reports CPI(M) saying the CM forced it; Janeesh said he posted as Youth
  Congress president, that his office removed it because circulating it
  under a minister's name could cause misunderstanding, and denied the CM
  asked.
- **Sept 30:** A Youth Congress march led by Janeesh, demanding the CEC's
  resignation, clashed with SFI near the University hostel in
  Thiruvananthapuram — each side blamed the other (stones and bottles
  versus a flaming torch) — and police entered the hostel, used a
  lathi-charge and reportedly tear gas. CPI state secretary Binoy Viswam
  alleged police were lenient with the Youth Congress and used excessive
  force on students. The same day, Yuva Morcha workers stopped Minister
  K.M. Shaji's car at Vanross Junction (he was reportedly without a pilot
  vehicle or escort), blocked the autorickshaw he switched to, and he left
  on foot — after about five minutes (The South First) or about half an
  hour (Madhyamam, Times Kerala) — in protest at the Youth Congress's
  black-flag demonstration.
- **Oct 1:** DYFI workers blocked Janeesh's vehicle in Kannur and
  Kasaragod; Taliparamba police booked six DYFI/SFI activists (damage
  reported at ₹2,000). In Palayam, police registered cases against 80
  SFI/DYFI and 80 Youth Congress/KSU activists, both under non-bailable
  sections per police. Cantonment police registered a case on their own
  over the Shaji blockade under non-bailable sections (section numbers not
  reported), and two Yuva Morcha office-bearers — district vice-president
  Peter Solomon and a second leader, Ranjith (described as general
  secretary or secretary) — were arrested and remanded.
- **Oct 3:** BJP state president Rajeev Chandrasekhar gave the Governor a
  memorandum seeking withdrawal of his "pleasure" over Janeesh, over the
  Youth Congress protest against Shah (ThePrint, UNI, The Statesman).
- **Oct 5:** CPI(M) state secretary M.V. Govindan said the UDF government
  had "surrendered to BJP" and that SFI/DYFI activists are booked under
  non-bailable offences while "no such stringent provisions are invoked
  against" Yuva Morcha and ABVP.

**Assessment:** the one-sidedness claim is an allegation by CPI(M) and CPI.
The reported record so far doesn't show a clear one-sided pattern: cases
and non-bailable sections were applied to both the Youth Congress and
SFI/DYFI at Palayam, and non-bailable sections were reported in the Yuva
Morcha case too, with two leaders arrested and remanded. Since section
numbers aren't published, comparative severity can't be judged. No source found mentions the RSS. Separately, Home Minister Chennithala ordered a
fresh Vigilance probe into the CEC around October 3–4 over a 2006
public-works matter (The South First calls it the Home Department's order),
on a complaint by Malayinkeezhu Venugopal — described as an aide to
Chennithala by The South First and as a KPCC leader elsewhere. An RTI reply
reportedly said the earlier case was closed as no irregularities were
noticed, while Venugopal and Chennithala say it was "hushed up." CPI(M)'s
T.M. Thomas Isaac welcomed the probe on October 5.
