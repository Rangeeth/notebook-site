# UDF Government Controversies — Keralam (2026)
{: .no_toc }

## Summary

A running, chronological log of controversies and criticism directed at the
UDF government (CM V.D. Satheesan) since it took office after the 2026
Kerala assembly election. Most entries describe allegations made by the
opposition, critics, or press reporting — not established fact or court
findings — so read descriptions as "X alleged Y", not "Y happened".

#### 1. Pre-result Mangalore chartered flight row

*Early May 2026*

Before the assembly election results were declared, opposition parties
alleged that V.D. Satheesan took a private chartered flight to Mangalore for
undisclosed meetings with senior NDA and Adani Group figures, prompting
collusion accusations. Reported specifics: a passenger manifest for a May 1,
2026 charter from Kochi to Mangaluru listed just two passengers — Satheesan
and K. Rejikumar. In Mangaluru, Satheesan reportedly met JD(S) leaders (the
BJP's Karnataka ally) at a reception, allegedly without informing the
Karnataka Congress leadership beforehand, as is customary for an
out-of-state leader's visit; photos of the meeting later circulated on
social media. One outlet additionally reported that Rejikumar, his
co-passenger, had separately been questioned by the SIT investigating the
Sabarimala temple gold theft case (the same case behind
[entry #8](#pradeep-pleader-resignation)) — a claim carried by a single source
and not independently corroborated here.

The row resurfaced later. PTI (July 4) quoted CPI(M)'s K.K. Ragesh alleging
that "the results" of the meeting with Adani officials were beginning to
emerge and asking who bore the cost, and The Hindu (September 22) quoted
CPI(M)'s P. Rajeeve saying Satheesan still had not disclosed who sponsored
the flight. The reports differ on its date — May 1 per the manifest reports,
the eve of the UDF government's swearing-in per The Hindu — and the alleged
meetings with Adani officials remain allegations by CPI(M).

#### 2. Cabinet formation & regional imbalance row

*May 18–19, 2026*

Soon after the UDF took office, haggling over ministerial portfolios ran on
past the May 18 swearing-in. The Hindu (May 19) reported that the UDF had
hinted at a broad agreement but the portfolios had not been gazetted: MLA-elect
Mani C. Kappan said allies were restive over "unresolved questions" and
questioned a term-sharing formula with "unusual" exceptions, notably for
C.P. John; and, according to a UDF insider, the allocation was stuck over the
Latin Catholic Church's insistence that the Congress keep the Fisheries
portfolio, tentatively promised to the IUML, with the IUML willing to give it
up in return for Higher Education. CM Satheesan went to the Thiruvananthapuram
Archbishop's residence hours after the swearing-in, reportedly to placate the
Church, which objected to no member of the coastal community being in the
Cabinet. The Hindu also reported anger within the UDF's rank and file over the
regional balance, with some districts unrepresented while Kollam got three
ministers; the CM's office promised a "consensus decision." Onmanorama adds
that the Latin community has four UDF MLAs but no minister and that Church
representatives stayed away from the swearing-in, and Outlook (May 19) that
the IUML got five posts.

#### 3. Minister Sunny Joseph's brother-in-law named to his personal staff

*June 2–8, 2026*

Electricity Minister and KPCC president Sunny Joseph faced nepotism
allegations after his brother-in-law Benny Thomas — husband of Joseph's
sister, a Kannur DCC office-bearer and former Ulikkal panchayat president —
was appointed his Additional Private Secretary in early June. CPI(M)
supporters compared it to the resignations of E.P. Jayarajan (2016) and K.T.
Jaleel (2021) over relatives' appointments; the minister's office cited
Thomas's political record. Thomas resigned on June 7, saying he did not want
his presence in the minister's staff to "cast a shadow over Mr. Joseph's
public life," and on June 8 Joseph said there were "no legal issues" and
that Thomas had stepped down solely to avoid controversy, while declining to
say whether the CM had asked him to resign (The Hindu, June 8). At a KPCC
meeting M.M. Hassan reportedly said great caution should have been exercised
in appointing relatives to personal staff. CPI(M)'s M.V. Jayarajan alleged
that nearly ten close relatives of Joseph had secured positions in
cooperative and other institutions over three decades through political
influence; that is an allegation, and no independent confirmation was found.

#### 5. Vehicle modification promise vs. High Court action

*May 14 – August 26, 2026*

At his first press conference as Chief Minister-designate on May 14,
Satheesan answered a question on his campaign promise to permit safe vehicle
modifications with a grin that went viral as his "pookie face," saying "if
promised, it will be delivered." On June 4 the Motor Vehicles Department
published a list of 18 modifications that could be made without prior
permission — mostly accessories already in common use, such as seat covers,
floor mats, door visors and dashcams — which drew mockery and disappointment
among enthusiasts; Progressive Techies' state president Aneesh Panthalani
called the promise one meant to impress youth and unlikely to be met.
Transport Commissioner C.H. Nagaraju said the list merely codified existing
practices and that changes to central motor vehicle rules need the Centre's
approval; lawyer Kaleeswaram Raj called the promise "not merely populist but
also deceptive"; Transport Minister C.P. John was non-committal, saying
there had been no ministerial-level discussion, and The Hindu (June 12)
noted that no policy-level steps had been taken. After a High Court order
prompted by a modified vehicle seen in flood rescue work at Adoor in early
August, the MVD stepped up its crackdown on August 24–26: ₹5,000 for each
unauthorised modification, vehicles to be restored before re-inspection, and
RTOs to report on enforcement by September 3 (The Hindu, August 25). That
August report says the government allowed certain modifications under
restricted conditions in June, while the June report describes the list as
the MVD's proposal for the government's consideration; LiveLaw reports that
the Division Bench held the ₹5,000 fine applies per alteration, continuing
an order of November 21, 2025.

#### 6. Wayanad township: officer transfers, and Congress's own Wayanad house fund
{: #wayanad-township-and-fund }

*Transfers June 9, 2026; slowdown alleged July 30; fund questions February–June 2026*

Two separate claims with different levels of support.

**Officer transfers and pace.** A June 9 IAS reshuffle (reported by Indian
Mandarins; no second outlet was found for its text) moved Suhas S (previously
managing director of the Roads and Bridges Development Corporation and
Special Officer for the Wayanad Township) to Special Secretary, Water
Resources, and Dr Arun J.O. (previously Chief Operating Officer of the
Township Project) to Director, Survey and Land Records. Arun J.O.'s entry
reportedly also lists the Special Officer, Wayanad Township Project role
among his additional charges (from a search-result summary; not confirmed on
the page that was opened), while Suhas S's says only that he keeps "his
other
additional charge." So at least two project-linked officers were moved; no
others were found, so "many" is unsupported.

On pace, Opposition Leader Pinarayi Vijayan alleged on July 30 that work had
slowed significantly under the UDF: he said 308 of the 410 houses had been
concreted before the change of government but only nine more in the UDF's
first 70 days, blamed in part on "unnecessary transfers" of officials with
critical responsibilities, and said 149 families were still waiting (Awaz
The Voice). No government reply and no independent count of houses concreted
were found, so this is an allegation, not an established fact. The baseline
was already behind schedule: on March 28, under the LDF and during the
Model Code of Conduct, Onmanorama reported more than 128 of the 178
first-phase houses inaugurated on March 1 still incomplete, with an official
hoping for completion by April 15. That exact count is single-sourced; The
News Minute (April 3) and IANS (April 17) describe the same picture with
different details — plumbing and infrastructure unfinished, only about 40
houses ready per IANS, and a missed pre-Vishu promise. On September
10, Agriculture Minister T. Siddique said 178 keys had been handed over
with 177 families living there, about 110 more houses were due by
end-September, all 410 houses by October 31 and amenities by December, with
roughly 810 workers on site and a prepared weekly work schedule; he blamed
rain for slight delays in painting (Onmanorama and Deccan Chronicle). The
end-September target for about 110 houses has not been independently
confirmed as met.

**Congress's Wayanad house fund — a party matter, not a government one.**
This predates the UDF taking office. The Congress and Youth Congress had
promised 100 houses for landslide survivors and run a fundraising drive;
the Congress acquired about 3.25 acres at Kunnampatta and laid the
foundation stone on February 26 (The News Minute, Deccan Chronicle);
Onmanorama described the project as having "nothing materialised" as of
February 9, and by April only preliminary work had been done (The News
Minute). In
April, KPCC president Sunny Joseph disclosed ₹5.38 crore received in a
joint account held with Satheesan, plus ₹1.05 crore collected by the Youth
Congress (reports differ on whether the latter is included in the former),
spent on land in two phases — about 3 acres 24.5 cents for ₹3.68 crore and
2 acres 15 cents for a little over ₹2.5 crore — with ₹97 lakh added from
KPCC funds (The South First instead describes phase 1 as covering over five
acres, so acreage reports differ). CPI(M) questioned whether the figures
matched the scale of the
campaign (The Federal, April 7). More specific allegations — for instance
that the account is missing from candidates' election
affidavits and the AICC balance sheet — come from a Deshabhimani report that
couldn't be opened, so they are unverified here. No finding of
misappropriation by any authority was found. CPI(M) was still questioning
the
Congress's housing delay in early June, calling the February foundation-stone
ceremony a face-saving exercise (Hans India, June 6).

#### 7. Nipah response row & "Eranamkettavan" remark

*June 11–22, 2026*

A single Nipah case was confirmed in Kozhikode on June 11 — a 43-year-old
man from Feroke. WHO's June 25 notice lists one confirmed case, no deaths,
no secondary cases and 104 contacts under monitoring; the patient recovered
and was discharged in early August. The opposition LDF nonetheless accused
the government of failing to control the spread of diseases (Nipah, Shigella
and other viral infections) and walked out of the Assembly on June 22 after
Speaker Thiruvanchoor Radhakrishnan disallowed an adjournment motion moved
by CPI(M)'s P.A. Mohamed Riyas and CPI's G.R. Anil, citing the minister's
explanation of the steps taken. Pinarayi Vijayan accused the Health Minister
of being absent from Kozhikode after the case was reported, said the
minister himself had spoken of a lack of coordination between the Health and
Local Self-Government departments, and pointed to vacant District Medical
Officer posts in five districts and 390 doctors awaiting appointment. Health
Minister K. Muraleedharan said the government was trying to right a health
system in shambles, accused the previous LDF government of neglecting
disease prevention and cleaning, said the single case had been contained
through effective tracking, and said some officials, including the Director
of Health Services, were transferred because they continued to show
allegiance to the previous government (The Hindu, June 22). He also drew
criticism, having said it was more practical to coordinate from
Thiruvananthapuram than Kozhikode, when an earlier speech from his time in
opposition resurfaced on social media — in which he said, roughly, that the
land would be ruined if an "unlucky" man (*eranamkettavan*) ruled it,
alongside a remark about previously unheard-of diseases; he later clarified
it. Separately, ThePrint reported three Shigella deaths in Kozhikode (two
children aged 3 and 4, and a 59-year-old woman) among 135 confirmed cases
since January — unrelated to Nipah.

An earlier version of this entry said the outbreak had killed "close to a
hundred people including children." That was wrong: no Nipah deaths were
reported. The figure likely conflates the roughly 100 contacts traced with
fatalities.

#### 9. Women's Safety Pension on hold
{: #women-safety-pension }

*Payments withheld since April 2026; latest status found: June 29, 2026*

The Women's Safety Pension — ₹1,000 a month for women and transgender
women aged 35–60 in AAY (yellow) and priority (pink) ration-card
households who receive no other pension — was announced by the previous
LDF government on October 29, 2025 and launched on February 11, 2026,
shortly before the election (the first payment went to about 10.18 lakh
women). On June 15, Opposition Leader Pinarayi Vijayan alleged the UDF
government was undermining it: payments for April and May had been
withheld, and the Finance Department was holding up decisions on new
applicants. He said about 16 lakh women were receiving it, and CM Satheesan
himself gave 16.4 lakh (against a projected 31.34 lakh) at the Assembly on
June 29 — both numbers come from the politicians; no neutral government
count was found. (The 31.34 lakh figure is the number the LDF government
projected as eligible, so "31 lakh women's pension stopped" overstates how
many were actually being paid.)

On June 22, CM Satheesan told the Assembly the scheme is not part of the
state's social security pension programme and remains under review, with
no decision yet on its future; payments had not been disbursed for the
past two months. The government says it never said the scheme would not
continue but can't decide without completing a scrutiny, and the CM accused
the LDF of rushing it out just before the polls. Quoting the CM in the
Assembly on June 29, ETV Bharat (Malayalam) reports the budget has earmarked
₹1,770 crore for the scheme, while IANS reports ₹1,950 crore — so the
figure is unsettled. No report after June 29 (English or Malayalam) was
found on whether payments resumed or the scheme was formally continued or
ended, so its current status is unverified.

**Context (calculated here, not from a source):** ₹600 crore is the
revised-budget allocation for Priyadarshini — about ₹50 crore a month —
and free tickets worth about ₹266.5 crore (government figure, to September
19) were issued in its first 100 days
(see [entry #4](../achievements/2026.html#priyadarshini-scheme) in UDF
Government Achievements). Paying 16 lakh women ₹1,000 a month would cost
about ₹1,920 crore a year. The two schemes serve different groups (a cash
transfer to lower-income women versus a fare subsidy open to all women and
transgender persons), so whether they compete as spending priorities is a
political argument rather than a settled fact.

#### 10. PM-SHRI education scheme U-turn

*June 17 – October 4, 2026*

The previous LDF government signed the PM-SHRI agreement on October 16,
2025. Education Minister V. Sivankutty described it on October 24 as a
"tactical" move to overcome the Centre's denial of school-education funds:
the state had lost ₹188.58 crore in 2023-24 and ₹513.54 crore in 2024-25 and

#### 11. Monsoon power crisis & mismanagement

*Mid-June – September 23, 2026 (background: April 28)*

Background: on April 28, in the LDF government's final weeks, the Additional
Chief Secretary (Power) authorised load shedding of up to 30 minutes between
6 pm and midnight "in extreme conditions" as demand hit records (6,033 MW
after 6 pm). Officials attributed that surge to a heat wave (41°C against a
forecast 38°C), heavier air-conditioner use and EV charging, and a shift to
induction cooking after cooking-gas shortages, which IANS and The Hindu
(September 16) linked to the West Asia conflict (the war on Iran and the
Hormuz disruption squeezed India's LPG supplies); Kerala depends on outside
sources for over 70% of its power.
KSEB said the authority covered only cases where the transmission and
distribution network came under "unbearable stress," and that no load
shedding had been imposed, though limited curbs occurred in parts of Malabar
because of line and transformer overloads. IANS reported that the LDF
government had claimed Kerala stayed free of load shedding "for nearly a
decade under Left rule"; that is the government's own claim, and no
independent confirmation was found.

Earlier years: reservoir storage was 32% in April 2026, the lowest in nine
years, but April 2017 was lower, at 24% (Kerala Kaumudi); June 2026's 21% was
the lowest for June since 2023. In 2024, with demand peaking at 5,646 MW
against a transmission system maximum then reported as 5,850 MW, the LDF
government ruled out cyclical load shedding, and overloaded 11 kV feeders
tripped automatically, causing localized outages (Onmanorama, May 2024). How
the 2017 and 2023 shortfalls were handled is not documented in the sources
reviewed beyond the LDF's own claim above, and no 2026 figure for the grid's
maximum capacity was found.

Unannounced evening restrictions (6 pm–midnight) were imposed from mid-June,
under the UDF government (Onmanorama). The reported causes then were on the
supply side: Kerala Kaumudi (June 18) cited reservoirs at 21%, availability on
the power exchange down to 5%, KSEB failing to secure short-term purchase
agreements, and repayment of electricity borrowed in March–April; Madhyamam
(July 16) cited storage at 29% against over 60% a year earlier, hydel
generation falling from 44.2 to 16.6 million units a day, and a reduced
central allocation, with Electricity Minister Sunny Joseph saying it was
impossible to predict how long restrictions would last. A DYFI protest at
KSEB's Thrissur office followed on June 19 (ETV Bharat, Malayalam). On July
21 the regulator, KSERC, directed KSEB to replace "surprise" cuts with
schedules fixed by 5 pm the previous day. CPI(M) held statewide protests from
late July over the power shortage (The Federal).

A second wave followed in September, with CPI(M) and CPI protests from
September 12–15. Onmanorama's September 10 analysis (single-sourced)
attributed a 900 MW shortfall to temperatures 2–3°C above normal, wet coal
cutting output at central stations, lower Idukki generation (1,384 MU against
1,680 MU a year earlier) and repayment of borrowed power; restrictions were
announced until the end of September. On September 23 the CM said
consumption had risen about 950 MW year on year and that the rise "had not
been anticipated or planned for in advance," and he pointed to a cancelled
25-year power purchase agreement with no long-term alternative in place; The
South First put the evening-peak deficit that day at 800–900 MW, with
September consumption about 925 MW above the same month last year, and
attributed the strain to demand up to 15% higher than a year earlier,
reservoir storage at 63.75% (80.29% a year earlier) with lower inflows tied
to El Niño, reduced availability in the national market, and unplanned
shutdowns of southern thermal plants; neither report discusses LPG or
induction cooking for September.

Context from The Hindu's September 16 explainer: only about 30% of Keralam's
demand is met from internal generation, mainly hydro. Rooftop solar capacity
of 2,259.50 MW (MNRE, August 30) lacks grid-scale storage, so KSEB surrenders
central and round-the-clock contract power by day and buys expensive power
in the evening peak; observers faulted KSEB for not planning for these trends
or building battery or pumped storage. The Grid Controller of India
anticipated a 12,000 MW national deficit on September 2, and a KSEB panel
had warned in July of further deterioration from El Niño. The Hindu calls
curbs persisting through the monsoon months unprecedented for Keralam, and
has the CM putting September demand about 925 MW above last year (94.44 MU
consumed on September 14, of which 70.82 MU was imported). The dispute over
465 MW of 25-year power agreements (Jhabua Power, Jindal Power, Jindal India
Thermal Power) cuts both ways: they were signed in 2014 under the UDF
government and rejected by the regulator in May 2023, during the LDF
government, for procedural violations. The LDF tried to restore them without
success, and the UDF argues that restoring them would have averted the
present crisis. KSEB's power purchases cost about ₹12,750–12,980 crore a year
in 2023-24 and 2024-25.
{: .note }
The 950 MW is a year-on-year rise in consumption, not an evening demand
surge, as an earlier version of this entry described it. The "first in a
decade" framing comes from the LDF government's own claim above and is not
independently verified.

#### 16. Vizhinjam Port 49% stake sale to MSC

*June 30 – July 2026*

Adani Ports and SEZ told SEBI on June 29 (reported June 30) that it had entered
a definitive agreement to sell its 49% stake in Adani Vizhinjam Ports Private
Limited (AVPPL), the port's concessionaire, to MSC's terminal arm (TiL) for
$1.397 billion (about ₹13,220 crore). Under the 2015 concession agreement,
Adani must keep at least 51% during construction and the first year of
operations and 26% afterwards, and a transfer of 25% or more of the
concessionaire's equity needs the state government's prior approval, which
Pinarayi Vijayan says rests on national-security and public-interest grounds.
Adani argues the port's second year of operations lets it dilute up to 74% and
that the process, which also needs central and Competition Commission
approvals, takes three to six months. The state government and the Opposition
say prior approval was not honoured when the preliminary understanding was
reached; Adani says it informed the Ports Secretary and the managing director
of Vizhinjam International Seaport Limited, but not the CM.

On July 1 Pinarayi raised the sale in the Assembly, saying the state had not
been consulted. CM Satheesan said the government had received neither a
request for prior approval nor formal intimation from Adani, and conveyed its
displeasure to Adani Ports, saying it would decide only after a detailed
examination and aimed to "fully protect Kerala's interests." Pinarayi
welcomed that stance but warned of a private monopoly, and on July 2 said the
transfer violated the concession agreement. By July 3 Adani's proposal had
gone to the Law Department and was to go to an Empowered Committee headed by
the Chief Secretary, with the Cabinet to decide. Pinarayi then wrote to SEBI
(reported July 5–6), alleging Adani failed to disclose the deal to the state
despite the agreement and asking SEBI to seek an explanation under Regulation
30(11) of the LODR Regulations and begin proceedings if violations are found.
He argued that a 49% stake clearly exceeds the 25% threshold and that a
dominant shipping line as shareholder could deter rival carriers and weaken
the port's appeal as a neutral transshipment hub. On July 4, CPI(M)'s Kannur

of IAS officer Divya S. Iyer as the port's managing director soon after the

committee consisted of officials from departments under the CM, who also
holds the ports and finance portfolios, and questioned the CM's Mangaluru
flight and who paid for it; these are allegations (The Hindu/PTI, July 4).
Adani says the sale would let AVPPL raise about ₹13,220 crore for expansion;
the state has spent about ₹4,000 crore on the port and its connectivity.

#### 27. Government hospital medicine shortage & private health lobby row

*September 22 – October 5, 2026*

On September 26 Opposition Leader Pinarayi Vijayan alleged that government
hospitals faced an "unprecedented" shortage, even of paracetamol, IV fluids
and anti-rabies vaccines, with stocks of 209 categories of medicines and
equipment for this financial year yet to reach hospitals and acute shortages
of antibiotics, cardiac medicines and doxycycline. He pointed to frequent
changes at the helm of KMSCL and failures in tendering and purchase orders,
and accused the UDF of a deliberate conspiracy to help the "private sector
health care lobby" by turning government hospitals into "show pieces" (The
Hindu, September 26; Deccan Chronicle adds an allegation of Congress funding
from private hospital chains). At a review meeting that day, KMSCL's general
manager said essential medicines, including fever medication and antibiotics,
were in stock and that medicines already on order would arrive that week.
Health Minister K. Muraleedharan directed that procurement for the next
financial year begin in October and finish by the end of December, with
middlemen removed, imported-medicine agencies allowed to bid, only drugs
procured by at least two state governments bought, and purchase prices
published online. He also attributed the shortfall to a decade of inherited
LDF-era mismanagement (ThePrint).

{: .note }
**Update (October 4–5, 2026):** the shortage is acknowledged but its size
is disputed. On September 29 the minister said 18 medicines were short
and would be restocked within a week, after having claimed days earlier
that stock was sufficient; he also said "not all medicines will always be
in stock." The KMSCL general manager reportedly put the number at 28, and
the Health Secretary called it "largely a media-created issue" (The
Federal, October 4). The Federal lists the reported causes as delayed
KMSCL procurement, weak supplier interest in tenders, quoted prices above
last year's, orders awaiting quality checks, and KMSCL running without a
permanent managing director; former Health Minister K.K. Shailaja called
the situation "serious" and urged a competent IAS officer for the post.
By October 5, a single Malayalam-language report (Times Kerala; translated,
no second outlet found) said several medicines — including doxycycline,
diabetes drugs, anti-filarial drugs and children's vitamins — had begun
reaching warehouses, with about ten more, including aspirin, due by
October 15, which KMSCL promised would clear all pending orders; the same
report quotes the minister calling the coverage exaggerated and suggesting
pharmaceutical-industry interests were amplifying it. That October 15
target for pending orders is corroborated by Asianet. On October 4, PTI
and Onmanorama reported the health department will set up district-level
Medicine Availability Monitoring Teams. The structural fix is slower: new
tenders start in October and finish by the end of December, with medicines
expected by February 2027 (The Hindu, September 26; The New Indian Express,
September 27; Asianet).
On patient impact, The Federal (October 4) reports some hospitals resorting
to local purchases, while Onmanorama (September 22) adds that even
wound-dressing cotton was short — a detail found only there. UNI (September
30) reports that district medical officers informed the government that
hospital stocks were adequate and the shortage lay at KMSCL storage centres.
The Federal adds that people dependent on government hospitals are making
repeated, costly trips to private pharmacies. No data was found on how many patients are affected.

#### 28. Protest clashes and "selective policing" allegations
{: #protest-clashes-policing }

*September 26 – October 5, 2026*

A run of confrontations over the Youth Congress's campaign against Chief
Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar has produced allegations that police
treat the Left and the BJP's youth wing differently. Timeline:

- **Sept 26:** Youth Congress workers showed black flags at Union Home
  Minister Amit Shah's convoy in Kollam, reportedly breaching the security
  cordon (the BJP alleged a security lapse). Reports differ on what
  happened to the five Youth Congress activists involved: The South First
  says detained, Reporter Live and ThePrint say arrested.
- **Sept 28:** Minister O.J. Janeesh, who is also Youth Congress state
  president, posted video of the protest and then removed it. Reporter Live
  said the removal followed the CM's displeasure, and Onmanorama (Oct 1)
  reports CPI(M) saying the CM forced it; Janeesh said he posted as Youth
  Congress president, that his office removed it because circulating it
  under a minister's name could cause misunderstanding, and denied the CM
  asked.
- **Sept 30:** A Youth Congress march led by Janeesh, demanding the CEC's
  resignation, clashed with SFI near the University hostel in
  Thiruvananthapuram — each side blamed the other (stones and bottles
  versus a flaming torch) — and police entered the hostel, used a
  lathi-charge and reportedly tear gas. CPI state secretary Binoy Viswam
  alleged police were lenient with the Youth Congress and used excessive
  force on students. The same day, Yuva Morcha workers stopped Minister
  K.M. Shaji's car at Vanross Junction (he was reportedly without a pilot
  vehicle or escort), blocked the autorickshaw he switched to, and he left
  on foot — after about five minutes (The South First) or about half an
  hour (Madhyamam, Times Kerala) — in protest at the Youth Congress's
  black-flag demonstration.
- **Oct 1:** DYFI workers blocked Janeesh's vehicle in Kannur and
  Kasaragod; Taliparamba police booked six DYFI/SFI activists (damage
  reported at ₹2,000). In Palayam, police registered cases against 80
  SFI/DYFI and 80 Youth Congress/KSU activists, both under non-bailable
  sections per police. Cantonment police registered a case on their own
  over the Shaji blockade under non-bailable sections (section numbers not
  reported), and two Yuva Morcha office-bearers — district vice-president
  Peter Solomon and a second leader, Ranjith (described as general
  secretary or secretary) — were arrested and remanded.
- **Oct 3:** BJP state president Rajeev Chandrasekhar gave the Governor a
  memorandum seeking withdrawal of his "pleasure" over Janeesh, over the
  Youth Congress protest against Shah (ThePrint, UNI, The Statesman).
- **Oct 5:** CPI(M) state secretary M.V. Govindan said the UDF government
  had "surrendered to BJP" and that SFI/DYFI activists are booked under
  non-bailable offences while "no such stringent provisions are invoked
  against" Yuva Morcha and ABVP.

and non-bailable sections were applied to both the Youth Congress and
SFI/DYFI at Palayam, and non-bailable sections were reported in the Yuva

numbers aren't published, comparative severity can't be judged. No source

public-works matter (The South First calls it the Home Department's order),
on a complaint by Malayinkeezhu Venugopal — described as an aide to

reportedly said the earlier case was closed as no irregularities were
noticed, while Venugopal and Chennithala say it was "hushed up." CPI(M)'s
T.M. Thomas Isaac welcomed the probe on October 5.
