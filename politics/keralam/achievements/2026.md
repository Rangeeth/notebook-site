#### 1. ASHA and anganwadi worker honorarium hike

*May 18, 2026*

At its first cabinet meeting (May 18), the government raised the monthly
honorarium for ASHA (accredited social health activist) workers by ₹3,000
(from ₹9,000 to ₹12,000), and by ₹1,000 for anganwadi workers and
pre-primary school staff. ASHA workers had staged a round-the-clock protest
through 2025 demanding a raise to ₹21,000; the outgoing LDF government had
already raised the honorarium by ₹1,000 in October 2025 (₹7,000 → ₹8,000)
and by another ₹1,000 in its January 2026 budget (₹8,000 → ₹9,000), so
UDF's ₹3,000 addition is a genuine further increase, not the whole gap
being closed for the first time — and it still falls well short of the
original ₹21,000 demand. Onmanorama (August 25) reports the minister saying
the ₹12,000 was paid as the August honorarium, together with a festival
allowance, ahead of Onam.

#### 2. India's first cabinet-level Artificial Intelligence portfolio

*May 20–21, 2026*

In its first cabinet portfolio allocation, the incoming government created
a dedicated Artificial Intelligence portfolio at cabinet level — reported
as a first for any Indian state — assigning it to P.K. Kunhalikutty
alongside his IT and Industries & Commerce portfolios. The stated aim is
to push AI-based governance, innovation, and technology-driven development
across public services, industry, and the startup ecosystem.

#### 3. Operation Toofan — statewide anti-narcotics drive
{: #operation-toofan-launch }

*June 2026 – ongoing*

The UDF government launched Operation Toofan, a coordinated crackdown on
drug trafficking with three parts: "Toofan Strike" (enforcement raids and
seizures), "Toofan Warriors" (public/student awareness), and "Toofan Care"
(counselling and rehabilitation). Kerala Police described the launch as
one of India's largest coordinated narcotics operations, with over 1,500
cases and 1,000+ arrests in the first 72 hours alone. By September 1,
2026, cumulative figures stood at roughly 9,935 cases and 10,725 arrests,
with seizures including 21+ kg of MDMA, 1,154+ kg of cannabis, 4.7 kg of
hashish oil, and 524+ grams of heroin — including a single 12.8 kg MDMA
haul via a Malappuram-led investigation, described as the state's
largest-ever drug seizure.

{: .note }
The same operation is also the source of a controversy — the arrest of
Youth Congress activists alleged to be part of CM Satheesan's social media
team. See [entry #19](../controversies/2026.html#toofan-mdma-bust) in
UDF Government Controversies.
For a cross-checked look at criticism of the operation's design and
execution (privacy, treatment capacity, enforcement targeting), see
[Operation Toofan — Anti-Narcotics Drive](../operation-toofan.html).

**How it compares to the previous LDF government's Operation D-Hunt:**

| Feature | Operation D-Hunt (LDF govt) | Operation Toofan (UDF govt) |
|---|---|---|
| Core philosophy | Intelligence-led policing — systemic supply-line tracking as an ongoing police baseline | High-impact mission blitz — a multi-agency, high-visibility condensed campaign to disrupt street-level networks immediately |
| Primary nodal agency | Kerala Police, directly under the ADGP (Law & Order) | Joint coordination: Home Department, Kerala Police, and Kerala Excise, with central-agency support |
| Leadership | Under the Pinarayi Vijayan-led LDF cabinet | Launched under CM V.D. Satheesan and Home Minister Ramesh Chennithala |
| Operational scale | Statewide special drives, localized city-police raids, interstate tracking | Institutional interstate grid, with a dedicated IG coordinating across all 5 southern states |
| Financial/tech tactics | Standard physical seizures, courier interception, network interrogation | Digital tracing — following drug money via UPI, bank accounts, and dark-web monitoring |
| Community layer | Vimukthi Mission and "No to Drugs" school/college committees | "Toofan Warriors," with a phase-2 "Toofan Janajagratha Samithis" reaching into local grama sabhas |
| Key targets | Local peddlers, traditional rackets, major interstate smuggling routes | High-end commercial venues, five-star-hotel DJ parties, dark-web operators, areas around educational institutions |

#### 4. Priyadarshini free travel scheme for women
{: #priyadarshini-scheme }

*June 15, 2026 – ongoing*

The government launched Priyadarshini, letting women and transgender
persons ride KSRTC ordinary-class buses free statewide, with no
application, registration, or ID needed — a conductor issues a zero-fare
ticket on request. The Transport Department order was issued June 11 and
took effect June 15; the revised state budget four days later set aside
₹600 crore for it. After the first month (June 15 – July 15), Transport
Minister C.P. John reported 3.81 crore free journeys and women's share of
ridership at 66% (IANS, ThePrint and Deccan Chronicle; only IANS also gives
the earlier 50.9% starting share). At the 100-day mark the government
released figures of about 12.54 crore free journeys and ₹266.53 crore in
free tickets as of September 19, with an average of about 12.93 lakh women a
day (Mediaone, Times Kerala); Deccan Chronicle and Metro Vaartha give 12.62
crore journeys and 12.88 lakh a day. Onmanorama's September 22 report gave
higher round numbers (13 crore journeys, ₹275 crore) that don't match the
others — Manorama News's own report that day gave 12.54 crore and ₹266.5
crore — so those are not used here. The 15.53 lakh peak on September 7 and a
15-day reimbursement cycle (with the Kerala Financial Corporation advancing
funds) appear only in Onmanorama; Kerala Kaumudi separately describes a KFC
overdraft with a government guarantee. No report of unpaid government
reimbursement was found for the scheme, though July reports
said around 8,000 KSRTC panel employees' salaries were unpaid, with workers
worried Priyadarshini would be blamed (those reports don't attribute the
delay to the scheme). It had been an "Indira Guarantees" manifesto promise
from the UDF's 2026 election campaign.

{: .note }
The scheme's cost is also being set against the paused Women's Safety
Pension — see
[entry #9](../controversies/2026.html#women-safety-pension) in UDF
Government Controversies.

#### 5. Ration card backlog cleared

*July 26 – August 3, 2026*

Under the government's 100-day programme, the Food and Civil Supplies
Department cleared 73,024 Priority ration cards against a first-phase target
of 50,000 — about 23,000 more. Onmanorama (July 26) reports the cards as
approved, out of 76,338 applications (1,353 returned for missing documents,
2,011 rejected), announced by Minister Anoop Jacob, with free supplies for
the new cardholders from August 3. IANS (August 3, via NewKerala) reports the
cards as distributed within the government's first 100 days, alongside 2,000
Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY) cards, and quotes the minister on clearing a
large backlog. The application breakdown rests on Onmanorama alone and the
2,000 AAY figure on IANS alone; a Hindu report of July 27 may carry the
breakdown but could not be opened.

#### 6. Operation Toofan 2.0 — phase 2 launch
{: #operation-toofan-2 }

*September 27–28, 2026*

Home Minister Ramesh Chennithala announced that Operation Toofan's first
phase (since June 2) had produced 12,584 arrests and 11,677 cases, with
seizures topping 24 kg of MDMA and 1,571.9 kg of cannabis, plus 10,295
awareness classes held. He claimed a 20–30% reduction in drug use and peddling statewide (some
headlines round this to 30%; no outlet tested it against data), crediting community participation —
particularly women "Toofan Warriors" — and said there had been "no
political interference" in the operation. Phase 2 ("Operation Toofan 2.0")
launched September 28 at Vengara, Malappuram, extending the campaign into
grama sabhas via Kudumbashree, Harita Karma Sena, and local self-government
bodies, and adding surveillance, ~35-hospital rehabilitation partnerships,
campus safety accreditation, and youth sports programs.
