# UDF Government Controversies — Kerala (2026)
{: .no_toc }

## Summary

A running, chronological log of controversies and criticism directed at the
UDF government (CM V.D. Satheesan) since it took office after the 2026
Kerala assembly election. Most entries describe allegations made by the
opposition, critics, or press reporting — not established fact or court
findings — so read descriptions as "X alleged Y", not "Y happened".
Last updated 2026-09-27.

### May 2026


<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 1. Pre-result Mangalore chartered flight row

*Early May 2026*

Before the assembly election results were declared, opposition parties
alleged that V.D. Satheesan took a private chartered flight to Mangalore
for undisclosed meetings with senior NDA and Adani Group figures, prompting
collusion accusations.

**Source:** [UDF faces criticism over ministers' alleged foreign junkets, Keralam CM's private-plane travel (The Hindu)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/udf-faces-criticism-over-ministers-alleged-foreign-junkets-keralam-cms-private-plane-travel/article71494511.ece)

#### 2. Cabinet formation & regional imbalance row

*Late May 2026*

Soon after taking office, friction broke out within the UDF over portfolio
allocation — allies objected that some districts (e.g. Kollam) got multiple
ministers while others got none. The Latin Catholic Church separately
protested the lack of a coastal-community representative in cabinet.

**Source:** [New Kerala cabinet: UDF portfolio talks run into headwinds as allies grow restive (The Hindu)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/new-kerala-cabinet-udf-portfolio-talks-run-into-headwinds-as-allies-grow-restive/article70996642.ece)

### June 2026


<div class="entry-card" markdown="1">

#### 3. K.B. Pradeep special pleader resignation

*Early June 2026*

The government appointed advocate K.B. Pradeep as Special Government
Pleader for the Devaswom Department, then faced backlash when it emerged he
had previously represented a company accused in the Sabarimala temple gold
theft case. KPCC general secretary P.M. Niyas and others in Congress
publicly objected, citing alleged Sangh Parivar ties; Pradeep resigned
within days.

**Source:** [Sabarimala gold scam row: Devaswom Special Pleader K.B. Pradeep resigns (The News Minute)](https://www.thenewsminute.com/kerala/sabarimala-gold-scam-row-devaswom-special-pleader-kb-pradeep-resigns)


</div>

#### 4. PM-SHRI education scheme U-turn

*June 2026*

The government resumed implementation of the Centre's PM-SHRI education
scheme, which the UDF had opposed while in opposition. The Left criticized
the reversal, noting only about a year remained on the scheme's original
2027 timeline.

**Source:** [Why is UDF government's stance on PM-SHRI debated in Kerala? Explained (The Hindu)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/why-is-udf-governments-stance-on-pm-shri-debated-in-kerala-explained/article71117264.ece)

#### 5. "Land Reforms 2.0" & asset privatization

*June 2026*

The revised 2026–27 state budget cut the plan outlay by ₹5,000 crore and
restructured KIIFB. Former ministers and opposition figures characterized
this as a corporate-friendly push toward privatizing public assets, dubbing
it "Land Reforms 2.0."

**Source:** [Privatisation, land deals, and gimmicks define Kerala revised budget, says ex-minister K. Rajan (The Hindu)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/privatisation-land-deals-and-gimmicks-define-kerala-revised-budget-says-ex-minister-k-rajan/article71125425.ece)

#### 6. Economy white paper backlash

*June 2026*

The government published a "status report" describing a structural fiscal
crisis inherited from the previous LDF government. The LDF responded with
its own white paper, calling the government's report a pretext for future
welfare cuts.

**Source:** [The UDF government's white paper on Kerala's economy: a diagnosis without an alternative (The Wire)](https://m.thewire.in/article/economy/the-udf-governments-white-paper-on-keralas-economy-a-diagnosis-without-an-alternative)

#### 7. Nipah maladministration & "Eranamkettavan" remarks

*June 2026*

A localized Nipah virus outbreak, reported to have killed close to a
hundred people including children, led to accusations of administrative
failure in containment. Health Minister V. Muraleedharan's response drew
criticism and revived past "Eranamkettavan" remarks attributed to him,
prompting an opposition walkout in the assembly.

**Source:** [Opposition LDF walks out of assembly alleging govt failure in controlling disease spread (The Hindu)](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/opposition-ldf-walks-out-of-assembly-alleging-govt-failure-in-controlling-disease-spread/article71132354.ece)

#### 17. Flood management private plane scandal

*September 2026*

During severe flooding in Malappuram, the opposition criticized Chief
Minister V.D. Satheesan for touring flood zones using a private plane
provided by an undisclosed benefactor, while declining to name the sponsor.

**Sources:**
- [CM Satheesan's chartered flight, "no expense to government," say government sources](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/keralam-cm-satheesans-chartered-flight-no-expense-to-government-say-government-sources/article71492287.ece)
- [Freebie row over Satheesan's chartered flight to flood-hit Malappuram dominates Keralam politics](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/freebie-row-over-satheesans-chartered-flight-to-flood-hit-malappuram-dominates-keralam-politics/article71502884.ece)
- [CM refuses to divulge details of individual who sponsored private flight](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/kerala/keralam-cm-refuses-to-divulge-details-of-individual-who-sponsored-private-flight-defends-ministers-foreign-trips-shajis-use-of-friends-car/article71498998.ece)
- ["Why not private favours?" asks Keralam CM Satheesan (The New Indian Express)](https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/kerala/2026/Sep/24/why-not-private-favours-asks-keralam-cm-satheesan)

**Legal angle raised by critics:** commentators and opposition figures argued
the non-disclosure touched on several statutes, while noting a court would
ultimately have to decide any actual liability:

- **Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988** — ss. 7 & 11: accepting a valuable
  service from an undisclosed source could be scrutinized as illegal
  gratification if a quid pro quo were shown.
- **RTI Act, 2005** — s.4 suo motu disclosure: since the tour was official,
  withholding the sponsor's identity was said to work against the Act's
  transparency mandate.
- **Representation of the People Act, 1951**: undisclosed high-value
  hospitality could bear on MLAs' asset-declaration obligations.
- **Ministerial Code of Conduct** (MHA): bars ministers from accepting
  valuable gifts/hospitality from anyone with whom they have official
  dealings, and requires avoiding conflicts of interest.

#### 20. Monsoon power crisis & mismanagement

*September 2026*

An unanticipated evening demand surge (~950 MW) forced KSEB into partial
load-shedding and unannounced power cuts (6pm–midnight) for the first time
in a decade. CPI(M) and DYFI protested what they called a failure to plan
for demand or arrange backup supply in advance; the CM acknowledged a lack
of advance planning.

#### 22. Government hospital medicine shortage & private health lobby row

*September 2026*

Opposition Leader Pinarayi Vijayan alleged that primary/family health
centres and district hospitals were missing 209 essential medicines and

LDF-era mismanagement and ordered a KMSCL overhaul, including removing
middlemen from the tender process.

#### 23. Thiruvananthapuram Zoo relocation & "Madison Square" project row

*September 2026*

The government proposed relocating the 167-year-old Thiruvananthapuram Zoo
from its 55-acre city site to Neyyar or Palode, redeveloping the site as a
public park modeled on Madison Square Park; a team was sent to study
Gujarat's Vantara wildlife facility. Backed by Mayor V.V. Rajesh (BJP) on
animal-welfare and public-health grounds, the plan was opposed by the
"Save Trivandrum Zoo" campaign, former mayor V.K. Prasanth (CPI-M —
alleging hidden commercial land interests), and former zoo minister
J. Chinchurani, who cited heritage value, veterinary/zoonotic risk to
native wildlife near the new sites, and the loss of a tourism draw
(~14 lakh visitors/year).
